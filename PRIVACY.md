# NoAI — privacy

*Published at https://h1tmakers.com/noai/privacy.html — that page is the canonical version, and the one linked from
the Chrome Web Store listing and from the extension. This file is its source.*

Three different groups of people appear here, and they need different answers.
**You, the user** come first. Then **the musicians and the YouTube channels
named in the filter lists** — they are not users of this extension, never agreed
to be on it, and have rights. The last two sections are for them.

## 1. If you use NoAI

**No account. No analytics. No telemetry. No error reporting. There is no NoAI
server anywhere — not one.**

NoAI stores, only on your own device, in the browser's extension storage:

- your settings (filter on/off, auto-skip, dim or hide, Content Credentials)
- your allowlist — artists and channels you have told it never to filter
- a count of tracks skipped
- a copy of the filter lists

None of it is transmitted, and it is deleted when you uninstall.

### Every network request NoAI makes

There are three, and no others.

**1. The Spotify filter list**, every six hours, from a static file on GitHub.
**2. The YouTube filter list**, every six hours, from a static file on GitHub.

Both are one-way downloads of public data files. Nothing about you, your
account, your listening or your browsing is sent with them. The files contain no
executable code; they are parsed as data and used only for lookups. As with any
web request, GitHub's servers see your IP address. We receive nothing and store
nothing. GitHub hosts and serves those files and is, for that publication, a
processor acting for us.

**3. Content Credentials — only if you switch it on.** See below.

### Content Credentials (optional, off by default)

Switching this on asks your browser for permission to run on every site. Exactly
what it then does:

For images large enough to be worth checking, NoAI reads the first 256 KB of the
image file and looks for a **Content Credential** — a declaration that tools such
as Adobe's, OpenAI's, Google's and Leica's write into the file itself. If that
declaration says the image was generated by AI, NoAI marks it.

- The read prefers your browser's cache, so it usually costs no additional
  network request — the image is already on your machine.
- When the cache does not have it, your browser requests part of that image from
  the site hosting it. That site sees a request similar to the one your browser
  already made to display the image. No cookies are sent. Nothing extra about
  you is sent.
- Results live in memory only, for the current browser session only, and only as
  "this image URL declared AI / did not".
- No image, no URL, no page address and nothing else goes to us or to anyone
  else. There is no server involved.

**So the honest headline is this:** nothing about you is ever sent to us or to
any third party, because there is nowhere for it to go. With Content Credentials
on, sites you are already visiting see one additional request for an image they
already served you.

NoAI reads the declaration; it does not verify the cryptographic signature. The
mark therefore says *declared by the file* — not *proven*.

Switching the feature off removes the site permission again.

### Retention

| What | Kept for |
|---|---|
| Your settings, allowlist, skip count, filter lists | Until you uninstall or clear them |
| Content Credentials results | The current browser session, in memory |
| Filter-list entries | As long as they are upstream and not on our retraction lists |
| Removed entries in the mirror's git history | Permanently — git history cannot be rewritten without breaking every clone. A removal stops the entry reaching users within six hours; it does not erase it from past commits. We say so rather than let you discover it. |

NoAI does not detect, block, mute, or skip advertisements.
NoAI is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or connected to Spotify or YouTube.

## 2a. If you are a musician on the Spotify list

### What is processed, and on what basis

- **Controller:** an individual, reachable at the address below. Identity
  disclosed on request to a data subject or a supervisory authority.
- **Contact:** noAI@h1tmakers.com
- **What we hold:** Spotify artist identifiers only — 22-character codes. The
  file we ship and serve contains **no names**. Names reach a user's screen only
  from Spotify's own pages. Where an identifier is linked to an identifiable
  person, it is still personal data, which is why this section exists.
- **Source:** [CennoxX/spotify-ai-blocker](https://github.com/CennoxX/spotify-ai-blocker)
  (MIT), which accepts public submissions. We did not compile it.
- **Purpose:** to let listeners who do not want AI-generated music see which
  tracks have been reported as such, and optionally skip them.
- **Lawful basis:** legitimate interests, GDPR Art. 6(1)(f). The balancing is
  published in [LIA.md](LIA.md), including the reasons it could go the other way.

### These lists are reports, not findings

Volunteer-curated, from public submissions, and they contain mistakes. NoAI is
built on that assumption:

- flagged tracks are **dimmed**, not hidden, by default
- **auto-skip is off** unless you turn it on
- any user can permanently allowlist any artist
- the wording is always "reported as AI-generated", never "confirmed"

## 2b. If you are a YouTube channel on the list

Everything in 2a applies, with these differences.

- **What we hold:** your channel handle (`@yourname`). A handle singles out an
  account and is treated as personal data.
- **Source:** [Override92/AiSList](https://github.com/Override92/AiSList)
  (CC BY-NC 4.0), community-maintained. We did not compile it and we do not
  decide who goes on it.
- **Two tiers, and they are not the same thing.** The source keeps them apart
  and so do we. If you are on the **blocklist**, your videos are dimmed. If you
  are on the **warnlist**, your videos are shown completely normally and carry
  only a small "possibly AI" marker — never dimmed, never hidden.
- **The criteria are more subjective than for music.** The source lists channels
  that "heavily rely on automation with minimal human creativity" or
  "mass-produce content using AI tools". Those are editorial judgements, not
  facts, and our wording says "reported", never "confirmed".
- **We did not inherit the source's removal policy.** AiSList states that
  removals are not handled with priority and that a channel stays listed even
  after it stops publishing AI content. **That is not our policy.** Ours is below.

## 3. Your rights, and how to actually use them

You can ask to be **removed** (rectification, Art. 16, and erasure, Art. 17), or
**object** to the processing (Art. 21). Write to **noAI@h1tmakers.com** or open
an issue at https://github.com/JayDK2/noai/issues.

We will remove you. We will not ask you to prove anything or argue the point.

- Removals go on a retraction list we control — `mirror/retracted.csv` for
  Spotify artists, `mirror/retracted-youtube.csv` for YouTube channels — and
  propagate to every installation **within six hours**. We do not wait for the
  upstream source to act, and we do not depend on it.
- **YouTube channels have a middle option:** if you would rather be moved from
  the blocklist to the warnlist than removed entirely, ask for that instead.
  Your videos then appear normally with only a small marker.
- Removal requests take priority over additions.

Please also consider asking the upstream source to correct its own copy, since
other projects use it — but that is not a condition for us removing you.

### Art. 14 notice

We obtained this data from public third-party sources, not from you. Notifying
each of roughly 29,000 people individually is not possible with what we hold —
the sources carry no contact details. Under Art. 14(5)(b) we therefore make this
information publicly available instead, which is what this page is. It is
published as an ordinary web page at https://h1tmakers.com/noai/privacy.html — linked from the Chrome Web Store
listing, from the extension itself, and from the repository — so an artist or
channel owner searching for their own name can find it without installing
anything, and without needing to read a file in a code repository.

You have the right to complain to a supervisory authority. In Denmark that is
Datatilsynet, https://www.datatilsynet.dk.
