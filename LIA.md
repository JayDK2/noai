# Legitimate interests assessment

Required because NoAI processes personal data (artist names) under GDPR
Art. 6(1)(f). Written before publication and kept current. Published rather
than filed away, because an assessment nobody can read is not much of a
safeguard.

## 1. Purpose test — is there a legitimate interest?

Yes. A growing share of music on streaming platforms is machine-generated, and
listeners have no way to filter it: Spotify ships labels on some AI content but
no setting that acts on them. Wanting to know what you are listening to is a
legitimate interest, and it is shared by the users who install this — it is not
merely our own commercial interest. There is no payment, no advertising and no
data collection involved.

## 2. Necessity test — is the processing necessary?

Partly, and this is the weakest link, so it is stated plainly.

Filtering by artist **identifier** would be enough for the extension to work.
Names are carried because a user needs to see who they are allowlisting, and
because an artist searching for their own name must be able to find out that
they are on the list at all. Both are user- and data-subject-facing reasons,
not conveniences for us. We considered shipping identifiers only, and rejected
it because it would make the list unauditable by the very people it names.

## 3. Balancing test — do the artists' rights override it?

**Against us:**

- The claim is a statement of fact, not opinion, and it is unflattering.
- The sources are volunteer-curated and demonstrably imperfect. Soul Over AI's
  own published data recorded no disclosure of AI use for 70 % of its entries
  and full disclosure for 1.5 %. That gradient does not survive into the
  identifier-only list we consume, so we cannot express it per artist.
- Artists did not consent and would not reasonably expect it.
- Being filtered out of a listener's results has real economic consequence.

**For us:**

- The data is already public — the upstream lists are open repositories.
- The default action is the mildest available: flagged tracks are **dimmed**,
  still visible, still playable. Nothing is removed and nothing is skipped
  unless the user deliberately turns skipping on.
- Every user can override any entry permanently.
- The language never asserts more than the source supports: "reported as
  AI-generated by a community-maintained list".
- Removal on request is unconditional, requires no proof, is implemented in a
  retraction list under our own control, and reaches all installations within
  six hours. It does not depend on the upstream maintainer.
- No profiling, no automated decision with legal effect, no combination with
  other data.

**Conclusion:** the interest is not overridden, but only because of the
safeguards. Take away dim-by-default, or the allowlist, or the unconditional
removal path, and the balance tips the other way. They are not features that
can be traded off later for a better-performing filter.

## 4. Review

Reassess if: the default becomes hide or auto-skip; a source is added that is
not open and correctable; the number of removal requests suggests a systematic
accuracy problem; or a supervisory authority takes a view.
